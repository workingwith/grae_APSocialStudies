Chapter 1: Living Things

Living things and non-living things share an ecosystem. Every organism has needs, and it can adapt to keep surviving when things change.

Key terms

EcosystemAll the living things and non-living things in one area.
NeedsThe things every organism requires so it can survive. extra Food, water, air, and shelter are examples.
MigrationMoving to another place to find better food sources and breeding grounds.
AdaptationA change that helps an animal survive and reproduce when its ecosystem changes.
MimicryLooking like another living thing.
PredatorAn animal that hunts other animals for food. extra
ResourcesThe wonderful things in God's world that we enjoy and use. It is our job to use them wisely.
Watch out: The word is ecosystem, not ecology. extra Ecology is the study of ecosystems.

How animals live in an ecosystem

  • Animals work together by forming groups to hunt or protect each other.
  • Some animals migrate to find better food sources and breeding grounds.
  • Animals defend themselves from predators with sharp claws or teeth, or with mimicry.
  • Animals must adapt to changes in their ecosystem so they can survive and reproduce.

Plants and people

  • Plants change an ecosystem for the better by producing oxygen and food.

People can hurt an ecosystem by

Polluting rivers.

People can help an ecosystem by

Recycling waste. Conserving water.

God has given us a beautiful world with many wonderful resources to enjoy. It is our job to use those resources wisely.

Chapter 2: Insects and Spiders

Insects and spiders are both small animals with hard outer skeletons, but their bodies are built differently.

Key terms

ArthropodAn invertebrate (an animal with no backbone) with an exoskeleton.
ExoskeletonThe hard outer covering of an arthropod. It protects the insect from being eaten.
MoltTo shed the old exoskeleton. An arthropod molts as it grows because its exoskeleton does not grow with it.
AntennaeInsect body parts used for sensing surroundings.
MimicryLooking like something dangerous, so predators think the insect is poisonous or tastes bad and leave it alone.
MetamorphosisThe big change in body shape an insect goes through as it grows up. It can be incomplete or complete.
NymphThe young insect in incomplete metamorphosis. It looks like a smaller version of the adult.
PupaThe stage in complete metamorphosis that comes between larva and adult.
Social insectAn insect that lives in a colony. It cannot survive alone; the colony meets its needs.
InstinctA knowledge or skill an animal is born with.
CephalothoraxThe spider body section where the head and thorax are joined together.
PedipalpsSpider body parts used to hold prey.
SpinneretsParts on the spider's abdomen that make the silk for its web.

Arthropods and insect design

  • Arthropods are invertebrates. Insects and spiders are both arthropods. extra
  • An insect has 3 body sections: the head, the thorax, and the abdomen.
  • An insect has 6 legs. Its antennae sense its surroundings.

How insects protect themselves

  • Exoskeleton: a hard covering that protects the insect from being eaten.
  • Camouflage: blending in with the surroundings.
  • Mimicry: looking dangerous, so predators think it is poisonous or tastes bad.
  • Speed: a fly zips away when you try to swat it.
  • Stinging or biting attackers.

Having soft, squishy skin is not a way insects protect themselves.

Ways insects eat

  • Chewing insects that eat plants by grinding have strong jaws.
  • Sucking insects have mouthparts almost like small beaks.

Insect life cycles

Incomplete metamorphosis (example: grasshopper)

  1. Egg
  2. Nymph
  3. Adult

The nymph looks like a smaller version of the adult. There is no pupa stage.

Complete metamorphosis (example: butterfly)

  1. Egg
  2. Larva
  3. Pupa
  4. Adult

Only complete metamorphosis has a pupa stage. The larva looks very different from the adult, like a worm or caterpillar. extra

Social insects

  • A social insect cannot survive alone. Its colony meets its needs.
  • Honeybee colony: the queen lays eggs. Worker bees gather food and take care of the young.
  • Ant colony: the queen ant's main job is to lay eggs.
  • An instinct is a knowledge or skill an animal is born with.
Watch out: On Quiz 3, the statement "The Bible uses the bee as an example of being wise" was false. The Bible points to the ant as wise (Proverbs 6:6). extra

Spiders

  • A spider has 8 legs and 2 main body parts: the cephalothorax and the abdomen.
  • Pedipalps hold prey. Spinnerets on the abdomen make silk.
  • Most spiders eat small insects and other small animals.

Spider instincts

  • Spinning webs: silk looks fragile but is quite strong, stronger than most man-made fibers.
  • Trapping: the trapdoor spider waits under a hidden door, then jumps out to grab an insect that comes near.
  • Fishing and hunting: fishing spiders catch prey near water. Tarantulas hunt on land.
  • Poisoning: sometimes a spider bites a person, but the poison of most spiders is not harmful to people.
Watch out: Quiz 4 said the brown tarantula and the black widow are both dangerous, and the answer was false. The dangerous spiders are the black widow and the brown recluse. Tarantulas are not among them. extra

Chapter 3: Plants

Flowers make seeds, seeds grow into new plants, and some plants can also start new plants without seeds.

Key terms

StamenThe flower part that produces pollen.
PistilThe flower part that receives pollen.
PetalsThe colorful flower parts. Their main job is to attract pollinators.
PollinationThe movement of pollen to the pistil.
PollinatorAn animal, such as an insect, that carries pollen from flower to flower. extra
Seed coatThe part of a seed that protects the young plant inside.
GerminationWhen a seed begins to grow into a plant.
RunnerA stem that grows along the ground and can start a new plant. A strawberry plant uses runners.
TuberThe underground part of a potato that can grow into a new plant.

Flower parts and pollination

  • Pollination is the movement of pollen to the pistil.
  • Petals attract pollinators such as insects. extra The insect carries pollen from flower to flower.

Seeds

  • The seed coat protects the young plant inside the seed.
  • A seed also holds stored food for the young plant to use.
  • Seed dispersal means seeds travel away from the parent plant. A dandelion's seeds are dispersed by the wind.
  • Seeds can also travel by water or by animals. extra

Plant life cycle

  1. Seed
  2. Germination
  3. Flowers grow
  4. An insect pollinates
  5. Fruit
  • A seed usually needs water, air, and suitable warmth to germinate.
  • After germination the young plant grows. Fruit holds new seeds, and the cycle starts again. extra

Reproduction without seeds

  • Runners: a strawberry plant sends out runners that grow into new plants.
  • Tuber: a potato tuber can grow into a new plant.
