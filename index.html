The Philippines: Location, Territory, Geography & People

Araling Panlipunan 4 · Reviewer and 60-Item Quiz (Lessons 1–4)

Lesson 1 — The Location of the Philippines

Key terms. Geography is the study of places, people, and environment. A geographer studies geography. Cartography is the art and science of making maps, and a cartographer is the person who creates them. Location is the physical position of people and places on Earth's surface.

Globe vs. Map.

  • A globe is a small, round model of the Earth. It shows continents, oceans, countries, and important lines, and — unlike a flat map — shows the Earth's true shape.
  • A map is a flat representation of the Earth showing features like rivers, roads, and distances.

Important lines on the globe.

  • The Equator (0°) divides the globe into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
  • The Prime Meridian (0°, through Greenwich, England) divides the globe into the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.
  • The International Date Line separates one calendar day from the next.
  • Meridians measure longitude (distance east-west); parallels measure latitude (distance north-south) and help show the position and climate of a place.

Special parallels and regions. The Arctic Circle, Tropic of Cancer, Tropic of Capricorn, and Antarctic Circle are special parallels. The tropical region (between the Tropics) gets direct sunlight; the Middle Latitude regions get oblique sunlight and experience four seasons (winter, spring, summer, autumn).

Types of maps.

Map typeWhat it shows
Political MapCities, municipalities, provinces, borders, roads, and railways
Economic MapA province's economic activity, products, and services
Physical/Topographic MapNatural features — landforms and bodies of water
Climate MapThe type of climate in a country or region
Population MapThe number of people living in a region or place

Parts of a map: title, compass rose, symbols, legend, scale, grid.

Describing location.

  • Absolute location — the exact location of a place, determined by latitude and longitude.
  • Relative location — the location of a place based on its relationship to another place, like its neighboring countries.

Lesson 2 — The Territory of the Philippines

Country, Nation, and State.

WordMeaningExample
Country / StateA place with people, land, government, and sovereigntyPhilippines
NationA group of people with the same culture, language, or historyFilipinos

4 elements of a state: Population (the people), Territory (the land, sea, and air it owns), Government (the leaders who make and carry out laws), and Sovereignty (the power to decide on its own without being controlled by other countries).

Territory includes: land, water, airspace, and subsoil and seabed (what's under the water and ground).

Bases of Philippine territory.

  • History — Treaty of Paris (1898), Treaty of Washington (1900), Boundaries Treaty (1930), and the Constitution of 1935.
  • The 1987 Constitution — our main law; it clearly states what belongs to the Philippines, supports the Philippine Archipelagic Doctrine, and includes Republic Act Nos. 3046 and 5446.
  • Presidential Decrees — laws made by past presidents to protect our territory (e.g., PD 1596 and 1599 of 1978, PD 9522 of 2009, PD 25 of 2017).
  • UNCLOS (United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea) — an agreement with other countries about the sea; it supports the Archipelagic Doctrine, which says all our islands and the waters between them belong to us as one whole unit.

Issues about Philippine territory: the West Philippine Sea dispute, illegal fishing (people from other countries fishing in Philippine waters), and fake land claims (maps from other countries that wrongly include Philippine islands).

Why protect our territory? To protect our natural resources, make sure Filipinos benefit from our land and sea, and show that we are an independent country.

Climate and weather. Weather is what the sky and air are like each day (sunny, rainy, cloudy, windy); climate is the overall, long-term weather pattern in an area. The Philippines has a tropical and maritime climate — warm all year (tropical) and humid and rainy because we're surrounded by water (maritime). We have two seasons: dry season (about November to May) and wet season (about June to October).

Winds and typhoons. Amihan brings cool, dry air from the northeast (November to February); Habagat brings warm, moist air from the southwest (June to October). Typhoons happen mostly during the rainy season. PAGASA (Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical, Astronomical Services Administration) warns us about typhoons and weather with rainfall warnings: Yellow (be aware), Orange (be prepared), Red (take action — danger).

Earthquakes, volcanoes, and the Ring of Fire. The Philippines is part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, so we have many volcanoes and frequent earthquakes. PHIVOLCS (Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology) watches volcanoes and earthquakes to keep us safe. Earthquake safety: Drop, Cover, Hold On.

Lesson 4 — The Human Geography of the Philippines

Human geography is the study of people and how they live in different places — their culture, language, traditions, communities, and way of life, and how their surroundings influence them.

Physical geography vs. human geography.

Physical GeographyHuman Geography
Mountains, seas, climate, volcanoes, riversPeople, settlements, languages, culture, livelihoods

Demography is the study of population. It asks: How many people live in a place? Where do they live? Is the population growing? According to the 2024 Census of Population, the Philippines had 112,729,484 people as of July 1, 2024. CALABARZON (about 16.93 million), the National Capital Region (about 14.00 million), and Central Luzon (about 12.99 million) together held about 39% of the country's population — population is not evenly distributed, since location and opportunities influence where people choose to live.

Ethnolinguistic groups. An ethnolinguistic group is a group of people connected by language and cultural identity ("ethno" = shared cultural identity, "linguistic" = language). The Philippines has over 170 languages and more than 200 ethnolinguistic groups spread across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao — including groups such as Ilokano, Kapampangan, and Tagalog (Luzon); Cebuano, Waray, and Ilonggo (Visayas); and Tausug, Maranao, and Maguindanao (Mindanao). Indigenous peoples have their own language, traditions, clothes, music, and beliefs.

Cultural diversity. The PSA's 2020 Census reported Tagalog as the largest reported ethnicity at 26.0%, followed by Bisaya/Binisaya at 14.3%, Ilocano and Cebuano at 8.0% each, and Ilonggo at 7.9%.

Types of culture.

  • Material culture — things we can see, hear, smell, taste, and touch (e.g., clothes, tools, food).
  • Non-material culture — thoughts, traditions, customs, values, language, stories, dances, and songs; these have no physical form and are shared and passed on through living them.

Geography influences people. Mountains may affect settlement, transportation, and farming. Fertile plains may support farming and larger communities. Seas and rivers may affect fishing, transportation, trade, and settlement. At the same time, people also change places — by building cities, roads, houses, farms, and industries, and by protecting or damaging the environment.
